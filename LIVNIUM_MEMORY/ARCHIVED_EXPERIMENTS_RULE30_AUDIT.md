## Crypto audit

### Custom AES-32 toy

`aes32_simple.py` implements:

- a four-byte key and block;
- four rounds;
- a 4-bit, 16-entry substitution table;
- a custom XOR mixing operation;
- a custom key schedule.

It is not AES. The README does call it simplified, but later language treats the
result as evidence about AES structure.

Fresh smoke result using its own disclosed example:

```text
key        12345678
plaintext  9abcdef0
ciphertext 163b5f7d
decrypted  1c375b78
match      False
```

The decryption path states that its custom MixColumns transform is
self-inverse and simply calls the forward transform. It is not self-inverse.
The script prints the failed match but has no assertion and exits successfully.

### Pairwise key search

`aes32_pairwise_search.py` uses:

1. byte-coordinate sweeps;
2. pairwise byte search;
3. Hamming distance from one known plaintext/ciphertext pair;
4. an exhaustive local refinement around the best candidate.

A fresh run recovered the fixed disclosed key `12345678` in about 15.36 seconds
after checking 555,863 local-refinement candidates. The README's 8–9 second and
100% wording is not supported by a multi-key, multi-seed protocol.

This is a legitimate search-programming exercise against a weak, broken toy
cipher. It does not prove AES leakage, geometric cryptanalysis, or a
cryptographic phase transition.

### Intended AES-128 implementation

`aes128_base.py` claims full variable-round AES-128, but its third ShiftRows row
is rotated left by one position, not AES's required left-three rotation.

Fresh 10-round standard-vector check:

```text
key       000102030405060708090a0b0c0d0e0f
plaintext 00112233445566778899aabbccddeeff
expected  69c4e0d86a7b0430d8cdb78070b4c55a
observed  b56e11e148de5e2eb6bcf009e584d0e2
match     False
```

Therefore the two-, three-, four-, five-, and round-sweep experiments do not
measure AES rounds. Their “quantum search” is ordinary classical coordinate,
pair, or local search.

### Crypto decision

Preserve:

- pair/coordinate search as a toy heuristic;
- Hamming-distance landscapes as an educational diagnostic;
- the broken known-vector result as a permanent regression test.

Retire:

- AES-32 as AES;
- the saved search as a cryptanalytic result;
- AES-128 round-transition conclusions;
- “quantum” naming for enumerative classical search.

A revival must first implement a correct invertible toy cipher and pass standard
AES known-answer tests before any search experiment is interpreted.

## N×N and N×N×N audit

### What is correct

For an odd `N×N×N` lattice, one lattice cell occupies the unique geometric
center. For even `N`, the geometric center falls between cells. A design axiom
that requires one distinguished center cell therefore selects odd cubes.

This is a clear and useful distinction:

- odd cube: natural unique center-cell anchor;
- even cube: no natural single center cell without an extra convention.

### What is not correct

The README also says even-cube parity causes rotations to break:

- exposure classes;
- class counts;
- symbolic weights;
- stable exposure cycles;
- Livnium invariants generally.

Those claims are false for the ordinary cubic lattice.

An independent checker enumerated all 24 proper cube rotations for `N=2,4,6`.
For every size, rotations were bijective and preserved boundary exposure `f`
and `SW=9f`.

| N | Exposure-class counts | Total `SW=9f` | Result across 24 rotations |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 2 | `f3: 8` | 216 | bijection, exposure, and total SW preserved |
| 4 | `f0: 8, f1: 24, f2: 24, f3: 8` | 864 | preserved |
| 6 | `f0: 64, f1: 96, f2: 48, f3: 8` | 1,944 | preserved |

The demo's `DataCube` intentionally omits Livnium fields and operations. An
implementation omission cannot prove mathematical impossibility.

### NxN decision

Preserve “odd cube has a unique observer anchor” as a design constraint. Treat
“only odd cubes are Livnium” as a definition of the Livnium architecture, not a
theorem that even cubes lack rotations or conserved exposure.

Retire parity-based rotation and symbolic-weight failure claims.

## Quantum-core audit

The capacity scripts create a large number of cells or recursively addressed
omcubes, then attach independent small `TrueQuantumRegister` objects or local
pair/graph correlations.

They do not create one `2,500,000`-qubit global wavefunction:

- individual dense registers remain small, usually at most ten qubits;
- many cells/registers are independent;
- “entanglement” in capacity tests can mean a stored pair or graph edge;
- deeper capacity runs explicitly skip quantum-state tests and count cells;
- the entanglement manager stores local relationships, not one global state.

The README's final honesty section correctly says these are classical
simulations, not physical qubits or quantum speedup. That boundary must govern
the earlier headline wording.

The AES application scripts use classical randomized sampling, local walks,
pairwise search, or lattice mappings. Terms such as superposition, tunneling,
collapse, and swarm do not change the underlying complexity.

A fresh run of `aes128_tension_calibration.py` found:

random-key mean        about 0.1266
repeated-byte mean     about 0.1245

The separation is a diagnostic of the chosen objective on a disclosed key.
The script itself notes that an earlier collapse merely found a repeated-byte
pattern far from the key.

The quantum implementation used by the runnable mirror is byte-identical to the
already audited `Desktop/core/learn/core/quantum` state-vector family.

### Quantum-core decision

Preserve the recursive addressing, capacity bookkeeping, and classical
local-correlation graph as applications of existing components.

Do not count this sibling as a new quantum implementation or as evidence for:

- millions of globally entangled qubits;
- quantum advantage;

## Ramsey audit

The source represents each complete-graph edge as a binary color and counts
monochromatic cliques directly. Search variants:

- score candidate colorings by violation count;
- flip edges implicated in violations;
- use local feedback, basin escape, curvature, or restart heuristics;
- checkpoint current/best state.

These are standard constraint/local-search ideas expressed in Livnium language.

### The four Phase-1 identities

For cyclic 3-gram frequencies ordered `f000..f111`, the archive records:

1. `f100 - f001 = 0`
2. `f001 - f010 - f011 + f101 = 0`
3. `f110 - f011 = 0`
4. `f000 + f001 + 2f010 + 3f011 + f111 = 1`

An exact symbolic check found:

- de Bruijn flow-constraint rank: 3;
- flow plus normalization rank: 4;
- every listed formula lies in the span of those generic cyclic flow constraints
  and normalization.

These identities hold for any cyclic binary row's 3-gram frequencies, regardless
of Rule 30. They are exact and useful, but their meaning is narrower than the
README claims.

The archive verifier checks whether a formula is identical or scalar-equivalent
to a single flow row. It misses linear combinations, which is why several
generic flow identities were described as non-flow discoveries.

### The real Phase-1 result: no 3-gram closure

An exhaustive focused search found two length-six cyclic rows:

```text
row A: 001011
row B: 001101
```

They share the same current cyclic 3-gram count vector:

```text
(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 0)
```

After one Rule-30 step their next 3-gram count vectors differ:

```text
A next: (0, 0, 1, 1, 0, 2, 1, 1)
B next: (0, 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1, 2)
```

Therefore current 3-gram frequencies do not uniquely determine next 3-gram
frequencies. This is a valid explicit non-closure witness and one of the
strongest mathematical outputs in the lineage.

### What the high decoder target actually is

`ChaosTrackerNBit` computes:

- `f_t`: frequencies of every cyclic N-bit window;
- `f_{t+1}`: the corresponding next-row frequencies;
- `c_t`: sum of `f_t` over patterns whose middle position is 1;
- `c_{t+1}`: the same sum for the next row.

For a cyclic row, `c_t` is simply the global density of ones. It is not the bit
at a fixed spatial center.

Phase 8/legacy Phase 9 train on thresholded `c_t` while their coordinate input is
a projection of a state containing `f_t`, `f_{t+1}`, `c_t`, and `c_{t+1}`.
This is reconstruction of a summary contained in the representation.

`real_world_test.py` comments that it wants a bit-for-bit next-center comparison,
but ultimately defines ground truth as `1 if c_t > 0.5 else 0`. Its saved 99.8%
is therefore not center-column forecasting.

## Preserved ideas

### AE-01 — Violation-directed local constraint search

Use exact local violation counts to choose graph-edge flips, maintain a best
state, and checkpoint long searches. This is a sound reusable heuristic pattern.
Its value must be compared with standard SAT/local-search solvers.

### AE-02 — Unique center-cell anchor

Odd lattice sizes provide a natural single central cell. This is a useful
architectural convention for observer anchoring. It does not imply that even
cubes lack rotations or conserved exposure.

### AE-03 — Toy coordinate and pair search

Coordinate/pair sweeps followed by local refinement can explore small discrete
landscapes. Preserve it as a general optimization toy, not quantum search or AES
cryptanalysis.

### AE-04 — Small exact quantum protocol regression

Teleportation and Bell/GHZ scripts are compact checks for a dense state-vector
simulator. They remain useful if always labeled classical simulation with
exponential register scaling.

### R30-01 — Cyclic de Bruijn flow identities

N-gram frequencies on a cyclic row obey incoming/outgoing substring-flow
constraints. These explain the exact Phase-1 identities and provide a correct
linear state-space reduction.

### R30-02 — Explicit finite-order non-closure witnesses

Search for two rows with the same current coarse statistic but different next
statistics. This is a strong, reusable way to prove that a chosen aggregate is
not dynamically sufficient.

### R30-03 — Pattern-frequency manifold

Represent a cellular-automaton row by local-pattern frequencies, then study the
feasible polytope/null space. This is useful descriptive geometry even when it
does not determine the next state.

### R30-04 — Exact sufficient-statistic baseline

Before training, derive whether the target is already a known linear or lookup
function of the features. The causal next-density label is exactly such a case.
This becomes a mandatory kill test for future Livnium experiments.

### R30-05 — Rollout horizon

One-step fit, reconstruction accuracy, marginal density, and autonomous
trajectory accuracy are different quantities. Always report first divergence,
multi-step accuracy, and compounding error.
