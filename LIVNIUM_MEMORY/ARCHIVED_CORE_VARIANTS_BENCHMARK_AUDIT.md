## 2. Executive conclusion

The useful material is architectural and diagnostic:

- trying a continuous spherical alphabet rather than assuming a cube;
- keeping SO(3) transformation code separate from state bookkeeping;
- representing a tetrahedral topology with an explicit finite rotation group;
- defining task constraints as inspectable tension functions;
- comparing a proposed optimizer with standard baselines;
- storing full assignments, objective values, steps, and result artifacts; and
- learning that a basin must own an immutable candidate state rather than merely
  point at coordinates in one shared mutable system.

The scientific headline claims do not survive independent checks:

- Livnium-O's cap-sum rule does not enforce non-overlap and does not recover the
  three-dimensional kissing number;
- its exposure quantity approaches one half, not one, and is better interpreted
  as an occluded cap fraction;
- its logged Hamiltonian potential and force disagree in the overlap region;
- Livnium-T's 12 listed matrices are not closed as A4, and system-level rotation
  is a no-op;
- its Bell-pair records are disconnected from node state and measurement;
- its recursion counts allocated copies but does not maintain a traversable
  global level index;
- dynamic basin updates violate the declared `SW=9f` conservation ledger;
- SAT/CSP candidate basins share one assignment instead of representing
  competing assignments;
- the saved SAT and CSP summaries count “winner object exists” as “solved”;
- the N-Queens search objective ignores diagonal constraints;
- Max-Cut stops after one valid partition, normally at step one;
- GSET signed edge weights are discarded while results are compared with
  weighted literature targets; and
- the saved benchmark does not support a solver advantage.

The historical 95.76%/96.07% SNLI result remains provisionally
leaked/unusable. Nothing in this family changes that classification.

## 3. Livnium-O: spherical prototype

### 3.1 Intended system

Livnium-O replaces the discrete cubic alphabet with:

- one spherical core;
- tangent spherical neighbors of arbitrary radii;
- a continuous exposure quantity;
- symbolic weight `SW=9f`;
- an SO(3) flow engine;
- a cap-sum “generalized kissing constraint”;
- a soft-potential Hamiltonian; and
- scripts describing crystals, shells, gravity discovery, and the
  three-dimensional kissing number.

This is a distinct design exploration and should be preserved as such.

### 3.2 Test boundary

Direct historical runners:

- `python tests/test_livnium_o.py -q`: 34/34 passed.
- `python tests/test_flow_engine.py -q`: 8/8 passed.

The 42 passing tests verify the formulas as implemented, core tangency, SO(3)
matrix behavior, ledger fields, and history-backed undo. They do not test
neighbor-to-neighbor non-overlap.

Running the folder through package-level pytest fails during setup/collection
because the hyphenated root and relative imports do not form a valid importable
package. The direct runners remain the correct historical test entry points.

Passing these tests establishes internal consistency for selected components,
not the external packing or physics claims.

### 3.3 Exposure law

The implementation uses

`f = (1 - sqrt(1 - (r/(R+r))^2)) / 2`.

For a unit core:

| Neighbor radius | `f` |
|---:|---:|
| 0.1 | 0.0020708 |
| 1 | 0.0669873 |
| 10 | 0.291701 |
| 1,000 | 0.477661 |
| 1,000,000,000 | about 0.49998 |

As `r` grows, `f` tends to `0.5`, not `1.0`. The documentation's claimed
`[0,1]` range and full-exposure interpretation are therefore wrong.

Geometrically this formula is half of the normalized spherical-cap expression
used for the portion of the core blocked by a tangent neighbor. “Occluded cap
fraction” is a more faithful name than free exposure.

`SW=9f` is implemented per neighbor, but the total is not `9N`. For six unit
neighbors the saved formula gives total SW about `3.6173`, whereas `9N=54`.
Some documentation asserts the latter while the source and tests explicitly
sum the former.

### 3.4 Kissing rule does not enforce packing

The implemented per-neighbor cap weight is

`w = 1 - sqrt(1 - (r/(R+r))^2)`,

and a collection is admitted when

`sum(w_i) <= 2`.

For unit spheres around a unit core:

- `w = 0.133974596...`;
- `floor(2/w) = 14`; and
- the implementation therefore accepts 14 equal neighbors.

The actual equal-sphere kissing number in three dimensions is 12. The README
also calls about 6 the “classical” value, which confuses the planar and
three-dimensional cases.

More importantly, a cap-area sum is only a scalar necessary bound. It does not
specify angular separation and cannot guarantee that neighbor spheres do not
overlap.

Fresh checks on the generated Fibonacci placements found:

| Neighbors | Accepted by cap sum | Minimum neighbor-center distance | Overlapping pairs |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 6 | yes | 1.63299 | 1 |
| 12 | yes | 1.15470 | 14 |
| 13 | yes | 1.1094 | 19 |
| 14 | yes | 1.0690 | 23 |
| 15 | no | not needed | not needed |

Unit neighbors need pairwise center distance at least 2. Even the default
six-neighbor configuration has an overlap, while the historical tests check
only neighbor-to-core tangency.

Consequences:

- “no overlap is enforced” is false;
- the generalized kissing law is not a sufficient validity test;
- the kissing-number scripts count radial contact with the core without
  enforcing satellite-to-satellite exclusion; and
- any claimed proof of 12 from those scripts is circular or incomplete.

### 3.5 Flow engine

The Rodrigues/Euler SO(3) rotation code is standard and useful:

- matrices are proper rotations;
- distances and core tangency are preserved under global rotation; and
- the history stack provides deterministic undo.

“Perfect reversibility” here means restoring a stored previous state. It is
valid engineering but not a newly derived physical reversibility law.

Moving a single neighbor does not revalidate all neighbor-neighbor distances,
so individual motion can retain or create overlaps.

### 3.6 Hamiltonian and force boundary

The Hamiltonian layer uses:

- pairwise soft repulsion;
- a compact neighbor-density kernel; and
- a target-density potential
  `0.5 * k * (SW - target)^2`.

Away from overlap, a finite-difference check showed the force matches the
negative gradient of the recorded potential to about `1e-8`.

Inside overlap, the density kernel clamps its value to one but still returns a
nonzero derivative. The force therefore is not the gradient of the logged
potential:

| Separation | Reported first-particle x-force | Numerical energy-gradient force |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.00 | 467 | 500 |
| 1.50 | 217 | 250 |
| 1.99 | -28 | 5 |
| 2.01 | -32.8575 | -32.8575 |

The mismatch is exactly 33 in the overlap region for this probe. That breaks the
claimed Hamiltonian/symplectic interpretation precisely where collision forces
matter most.

The “discover gravity” script fits a quadratic to the same target-density
potential already inserted into the source. Recovering the programmed spring
shape does not discover gravity from independent observations.

### 3.7 Livnium-O preservation decision

Preserve:

- the spherical-alphabet exploration;
- SO(3) utilities;
- history-backed undo;
- continuous cap and soft-potential experiments;
- the test suite as a record of intended invariants; and
- failed packing/gradient cases as regression tests for a future version.

Do not promote:

- cap-sum validity as a generalized kissing theorem;
- generated neighbor sets as non-overlapping packings;
- `f` as a `[0,1]` free-exposure measure;
- total `SW=9N`;
- fitted programmed potential as gravity discovery; or
- the current overlap dynamics as Hamiltonian.

A lawful rebuild needs explicit pairwise separation constraints, a continuous
potential whose derivative is used verbatim, and comparison against known
spherical-code/packing baselines.

## 4. Livnium-T: tetrahedral/simplex prototype

### 4.1 Intended system

Livnium-T defines:

- one core node and four vertex nodes;
- core `f=0, SW=0`;
- vertices `f=3, SW=27`;
- total SW 108;
- a nominal 12-element tetrahedral rotation group A4;
- local qubit objects and Bell-pair records;
- five-way recursive subdivision; and
- dynamic/multi-basin search over the five-node topology.

The five-node data model is clear and small enough to test exhaustively.

### 4.2 Historical tests and scripts

Direct results:

- `test_livnium_t.py`: 12/12 unit tests passed.
- `test_quantum_recursive_accurate.py`: completed and reported 155 node/qubit
  objects across 31 copied systems at depth 2.
- `test_recursive_depth_quick.py`: completed through depth 5.
- `test_recursive_import.py`: syntax checks passed.
- `test_recursive.py`: failed its relative import.
- `test_recursive_simple.py`: failed its dynamically patched package import.

The depth-limit script built:

| Maximum depth | Total nodes | Geometries | Time | Peak traced memory |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 5 | 1 | 0.008 s | 0.60 MB |
| 1 | 30 | 6 | 0.024 s | 0.05 MB |
| 2 | 155 | 31 | 0.124 s | 0.19 MB |
| 3 | 780 | 156 | 0.566 s | 0.86 MB |
| 4 | 3,905 | 781 | 2.844 s | 4.22 MB |
| 5 | 19,530 | 3,906 | 15.5 s | 21.01 MB |
| 6 | 97,655 | 19,531 | 82.1 s | 104.93 MB |

Depth 7 was interrupted rather than consuming more time and memory. The counts
match the allocation formula `5 * sum(5^i)`. That confirms eager exponential
copying, not useful quantum capacity.

The “accurate capacity” script creates independent two-amplitude objects,
applies a Hadamard to one object in each system, and explicitly does not
entangle the objects. Its 100% utilization number is allocation divided by the
same allocation count.

### 4.3 The listed matrices are not A4

All 12 stored matrices are:

- distinct;
- orthogonal; and
- determinant +1.

Those properties are necessary but insufficient for the tetrahedral rotation
group.

Independent action and closure checks found:

- rotation IDs 3 and 7 do not map the four canonical tetrahedron vertices onto
  the tetrahedron;
- each has maximum nearest-vertex error `1.154700538...`; and
- 50 of the 144 ordered pairwise matrix products do not equal any stored
  matrix.

The source comment describes rotation 3 as a 3-cycle, but its explicit
permutation is a four-cycle. The SVD “proper rotation” repair turns that odd
permutation into a valid SO(3) matrix that is not a tetrahedral symmetry.
Rotation 7 is the corresponding bad inverse construction.

The historical test checks only count, shape, orthogonality, determinant, and
individual inverse lookup. It never checks vertex action or group closure.

System-level `LivniumTSystem.apply_rotation()` then returns the same node
objects in the same mapping for every rotation ID. It records no positions or
permutations. Even a corrected matrix table would not currently transform the
system state.

Therefore:

- “12 SO(3) matrices” is verified;
- “the implementation is A4” is falsified; and
- “perfectly reversible system dynamics” is currently trivial because the
  system-level operation is a no-op.

### 4.4 Quantum layer is disconnected

The local `QuantumNode` implementation correctly supports normalization,
single-qubit gates, Born sampling in the computational/X bases, and state
collapse for one two-level vector.

The multi-node claims fail at the representation boundary:

1. `EntanglementManager.create_bell_pair()` stores a separate four-amplitude
   vector.
2. It does not update either participating `QuantumNode`.
3. `QuantumSystem.measure_node()` reads and collapses only the local
   two-amplitude node.
4. It neither consults nor collapses the Bell-pair vector or the partner node.

Fresh demonstration:

- nodes 1 and 2 began in local `|0>`;
- declaring a `phi_plus` pair left both local states at `|0>`;
- measuring both returned deterministic `0,0`;
- the pair record existed, but did not cause a correlated quantum measurement.

Additional correctness boundaries:

- the advertised CNOT/CZ/SWAP matrices are 4x4, while
  `QuantumSystem.apply_gate(node_id, gate_type)` always applies a gate to one
  two-level node; CNOT raises
  `ValueError: Unitary shape (4, 4) doesn't match num_levels 2`;
- the concurrence approximation reports 1.0 for the separable product state
  `|++>`, whose exact pure-state concurrence `2|ad-bc|` is 0.0; and
- the Y-basis probability signs are swapped relative to the collapsed-state
  labels: measuring `|+i>` deterministically reports outcome 1 and collapses to
  `|-i>`.

Geometry coupling initializes core to `|0>` and every vertex to `|+>`. Its
exposure-to-entanglement scalar is not used to create or evolve a joint state.

Preserve the single-node utilities and explicit pair graph as software ideas.
Do not call the saved layer a consistent multi-qubit simulator.

### 4.5 Recursive bookkeeping

The object tree is real: each of five nodes owns another complete five-node
system down to the selected depth.

However:

- `self.levels` receives only the root under key 0;
- children all reuse their depth as `level_id`;
- children are not inserted into the global index;
- `get_level(1)` fails even after building depth 1 or deeper;
- `get_level_sw(0)` recurses by depth ID through the incomplete index and
  fails; and
- `verify_recursive_conservation()` iterates the global index, so it does not
  validate every copied geometry in the tree.

Downward projection returns a constraints dictionary but does not apply those
constraints to child state. Upward projection sums counts and SW when explicitly
called. Claims about recursive entanglement, memory resonance, or new
cross-scale physics are documentation, not implemented evidence.

### 4.6 Dynamic basins violate the core ledger

The declared node invariant is exact:

- core SW must be 0;
- each vertex SW must be 27; and
- total SW must be 108.

Dynamic basin search directly increments or decrements `symbolic_weight` up to
50 without updating exposure or using a conserved transfer. A fresh correct
update to vertices 1 and 2 changed total SW from 108.0 to 108.2 and caused
`verify_ledger()` to return false.

`compute_local_curvature()` and `compute_noise_entropy()` are identical
variance-over-mean formulas under different names. The advertised
geometry-decided parameters still multiply explicit
`base_alpha/base_beta/base_noise` hyperparameters.

All basins mutate the same five node objects, so overlapping basins interfere.
The winning basin is selected from metrics of that shared state rather than
from independently represented candidate solutions.

The vertex-rotation policy can return true solely when a supplied stats
dictionary says `num_alive==1`, even when curvature and tension thresholds
fail. When locked, it returns identity. The Rubik analogy is narrative, not an
evaluated mechanism.

### 4.7 Livnium-T preservation decision

Preserve:

- the explicit core-plus-four-vertices schema;
- canonical tetrahedron coordinates;
- the idea of exhaustive group-law tests;
- exact base-5 encode/decode;
- small local state-vector utilities;
- tree capacity formulas and measured scaling costs; and
- the failed group/entanglement/ledger cases as future regression tests.

Rebuild before use:

- generate A4 from all even vertex permutations and assert faithful action,
  uniqueness, identity, inverses, and closure;
- store an actual vertex permutation or position state in system rotation;
- use one joint state vector/tensor-network object for multi-qubit operations
  and measurement;
- compute exact concurrence for supported states;
- fix Y-basis bra/collapse correspondence;
- assign globally unique tree paths/IDs and traverse the tree itself;
- distinguish per-level invariants from total-across-tree accounting; and
- keep immutable SW/exposure laws separate from mutable task scores.

### 5.1 Preserved artifacts

The family contains:

  instances;
- six JSON CSPs: N-Queens 4/5/6/8, blank 4x4 Sudoku/Latin constraints, and an
  unsatisfiable two-color triangle;
- ten GSET graph files;
- five saved result/distribution JSONs;
- two distribution plots; and
- solver, baseline, data-generation, download, and plotting scripts.

The 1,000 UF20 instances survive, but the saved SAT result file evaluates only
the 10 local `satlib/test` files. It is not a SATLIB UF20 benchmark result.

### 5.2 Common candidate-state flaw

The encoder says candidate basins represent variable assignments, but it stores
only coordinate lists.

For SAT:

- every variable maps to one coordinate;
- each candidate loop overwrites the same shared cells with a new random
  assignment;
- every appended basin contains the same full coordinate list; and
- after encoding, all basins see only the last shared assignment.

A fresh two-variable probe created five basin records but only one unique
coordinate set, all reading the same final `[15,15]` SW state.

CSP repeats the same pattern. Five fresh candidate records again produced one
unique coordinate set.

The SAT/CSP result decoders accept a `basin` argument but never use it. They read
the one global system. Constraint tension is also computed from that same global
system and then added equally to every basin. The basins therefore do not
compete as different assignments.

Max-Cut creates different three-coordinate subsets for its basin records, but
the decoded partition and every edge-tension field still read the same global
partition. A “valid partition” exists immediately, so the solver breaks at
step one without optimizing the objective.

This is the central lesson from the suite:

> A candidate basin must own a candidate state, or a reversible state delta.
> A coordinate set in one mutable global state is not a candidate solution.

1. The universal CSP tension encoder implements `all_different`, `equal`,

   ignores 6, 10, 15, or 28 diagonal constraints depending on board size.
2. Candidate initialization writes `domain_index * 10`, while decoding uses

   indices 0 and 2; for a five-value domain all multiples of ten decode to index

- median 19/29;
- population standard deviation about 3.93;
- range 19–28; and
- 28 of 50 runs at exactly 19.

A structured histogram of an objective that omits diagonal tension is not
evidence of useful geometric relaxation.

Preserve the problem formats, independent constraint checker, baseline harness,
and distribution-analysis tooling. Rebuild the state/objective mapping.

## 7. Ideas to carry forward

### O-01 — Pairwise-valid spherical alphabet

Keep the idea of a radius-aware spherical symbol system, but define validity
from all pairwise angular/separation constraints. A scalar cap budget may be a
cheap necessary prefilter, never the final packing test.

### O-02 — Energy/force single source of truth

Implement one differentiable potential and obtain forces by automatic
differentiation or by its exact analytic derivative. Add finite-difference
tests inside, at, and outside every clamp or piecewise boundary.

### T-01 — Faithful finite-group action

Generate transformations from the abstract group action, assert action on the
canonical vertices, and then derive matrices. Test closure, not only matrix
orthogonality.

### T-02 — Immutable law versus mutable task state

Keep exposure/SW laws immutable. Store learning scores, basin mass, confidence,
and reinforcement in separate fields with separately declared conservation.

### B-01 — Basin-owned candidate state

A basin needs:

- an immutable assignment/partition;
- or a reversible delta from a declared base state;
- its own objective and constraint violations;
- deterministic mutation/crossover operators; and
- independent decoding.

Shared coordinate lists are regions of one state, not alternative solutions.

### B-02 — Strict success semantics

Use separate fields:

- `candidate_produced`;
- `valid_solution`;
- `objective_value`;
- `optimality_gap`;
- `sat_witness_verified`;
- `unsat_proof_verified`;
- `timeout`; and
- `decision_complete`.

Never let `winner is not None` imply `solved`.

### B-03 — Objective-identity preflight

Before benchmarking:

- preserve all input weights/signs;
- recompute the objective independently from the saved witness;
